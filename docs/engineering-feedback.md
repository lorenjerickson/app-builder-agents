# Engineering feedback and evaluation efficiency

This guidance governs external engineering work on this repository. The reusable
agent definitions and evaluation harness are the products under inspection; this
file is not a candidate runtime contract or a new lifecycle gate. Follow AGENTS.md
and the user's selected scope. Preserve independent review and intentional feedback
between the engineering team and explicitly selected domain exercises.

## Select the outcome and keep authority separate

At task start, record the selected outcome and completion evidence in the existing
backlog entry or assignment: definition maintenance, definition publication, textual
behavioral evaluation, harness repair, or a domain execution exercise. A task may
explicitly select several; none automatically implies the others. A definition
release does not require a new pilot or all application-delivery gates unless the
user selected that evidence as a release condition. Report evidence limits honestly;
do not waive selected criteria or label static/textual evidence as executed delivery.

External engineering guidance governs changes to definitions and their harness.
Candidate approval and repair rules are subjects to test, not authority over their
own maintenance. Record domain-process outcomes separately from proposed reusable
process improvements. Neither reading these definitions nor referencing this project
makes its network the workflow for an arbitrary project. Transmission permission,
urgency, or permission to investigate a failure does not enlarge delivery scope.
Reuse clear authorization; do not add approval requests for routine work already
selected. Existing execution holds and real user approval boundaries still apply.

## Bound the selected cycle, not the intended network topology

Use the existing assignment/run record to identify engineering authors/reviewers,
experimental subjects/evaluators, the completion condition, and applicable limits.
For model-backed evaluation, record a finite planned dispatch/iteration bound and
available time/usage limits before dispatch. Include parent/helper activity and
native model calls inside scripts or adapters, including retries and evaluator
calls; a child-agent count is not a complete usage measure. Reserve room for review
and closeout. Carry remaining limits across handoffs and resumed tasks; do not reset
them by renaming work or changing helpers. Do not invent numeric account costs when
telemetry is unavailable: track call/attempt counts and report the uncertainty.

Check the remaining bound at material checkpoints, not on every tool call. Stop
before exceeding it and obtain the required scope/resource decision. Missing a
numeric account-cost estimate does not block read-only instruction maintenance;
it does not authorize unlimited model-backed execution either. Do not impose a
single-helper cap on a deliberately selected network experiment. Assign one owner
per artifact and distinct independent review responsibilities instead.

## Establish harness readiness before spending evaluation calls

Reuse prior readiness evidence for unchanged components. For a new or materially
changed evaluation path, preflight the complete request shape with realistic
synthetic responses before allocating real subjects. Check exact subject/evaluator
instructions, criteria, transport byte/character limits at every boundary, supported
model context and response headroom, isolation, timeout handling, and citation/
receipt integrity. Bytes are not tokens; disclose unknown provider limits. Prefer
local deterministic checks and use a bounded provider probe only when needed and
within authorization. Small fixtures alone do not establish full-request feasibility.

Classify failures before redispatch: candidate behavior, evaluation instrument,
transport/harness, or environment. Repeated infrastructure failures pause affected
candidate dispatch for a consolidated harness diagnosis and repair; unaffected
independent work may continue within scope. If scaffold creation or recovery is
itself the selected behavior under test, preserve its observed defects as candidate
evidence rather than pre-fixing the experiment. Do not silently change thresholds,
fixtures or subjects to obtain a pass. Retain original failed/inconclusive outcomes.

## Reuse evidence without losing independent judgment

Use an existing lossless compact representation where available. Factor repeated
source text and derived representations instead of dropping instructions, rubric
clauses or response content. Validate reconstruction, source identity and exact
citation boundaries with deterministic checks; do not recreate a proven adapter
for each run. Treat hashes as identity/invalidation aids, not proof of semantic
correctness or independent acceptance.

Bind responses and reviews to their inputs, source revisions, criteria, protocol,
model/settings and relevant environment. Reuse unchanged evidence only while those
bindings remain applicable. Invalidate every affected consumer of changed inputs,
regardless of its prior score; rerun that dependency closure and any selected
cross-cutting checks. Do not restart all subjects for an isolated change or replace
completed answers to obtain better scores. Retain failure and attempt lineage.
A budget/protocol amendment must be explicit, prospectively bound and reported;
changed-model or changed-protocol results cannot be described as unchanged-condition
comparisons. Reused or post-feedback cases do not establish held-out generalization.

Maintain one finding register in existing review/run artifacts: criterion, affected
artifact identity, cause, owner, required evidence and disposition. Author self-checks
support but do not replace independent semantic review. Reviewers retain judgment
over correctness, missing criteria and false passes; do not constrain them to the
author's stated defects. Rechecks focus on changed findings, affected dependencies
and regression obligations, reusing accepted unchanged evidence. A justified broader
review is allowed within selected scope; explain what new risk requires it.

Give each reviewer the bounded question, relevant changes, binding inputs, prior
findings and evidence references. Fetch necessary sections rather than dumping whole
historical packets/logs into every context. Preserve access to complete evidence.
Use scope boundaries to decide whether to continue a reviewer or provide a focused
handoff; do not spawn a new reviewer for every minor correction by default.

## Close out at the selected outcome

Record changed artifacts, independently reviewed findings, evidence reused or
invalidated, harness-caused retries, applicable usage/attempt observations and
remaining limitations in the existing task record. Distinguish tests of the harness,
textual behavior and actual domain execution. Stop at the selected deliverable;
new domain work or a fresh qualification run requires its own selection. Instruction
changes alone do not authorize running any of the checks described here.
